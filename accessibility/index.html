Accessibility

What we have done, what we know is imperfect, and how to tell us

Our aim

This site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA. It is built as plain, static HTML, so it works without JavaScript, without plugins and on old devices, and it stays readable in a browser with styles turned off.

What is in place

  • Semantic landmarks and headings, and a “skip to main content” link.
  • Text and background colours chosen for contrast; the site follows your operating system’s light or dark setting, and a button in the header lets you choose the other one. Your choice is remembered on your own device.
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation, visible focus outlines, and no reliance on hover or drag.
  • No automatically playing media, no carousels, no motion beyond a subtle transition — and that transition is disabled if you have “reduce motion” set.
  • Text that zooms to 200% without loss of content; layouts that reflow to a single column on narrow screens.
  • Alternative text on images, table alternatives for data shown as graphics, and a plain list of every map entry so the map is never the only route to the information.
  • A readable print stylesheet, so pages can be printed or saved as PDF.

Known limitations

  • The map. It relies on JavaScript and on third-party map tiles. The list below the map contains the same information and works without either. Map text cannot be resized independently of the map.
  • Third-party embeds. The contribution form is hosted by our own form endpoint on Cloudflare Workers and we cannot guarantee its accessibility. If it is difficult to use, email us and we will take your contribution by email or over the phone.
  • Scan quality. Some archive material we publish was digitised years ago from imperfect sources. Transcripts or descriptions can be supplied on request.

Formats and alternatives

We can provide any page as a plain text file, a large-print PDF, an Easy Read summary, or an audio recording of a story on request. Contact hello@example.ac.uk and say what would help.

Telling us about a problem

Email hello@example.ac.uk with the page and a description of what went wrong. We aim to reply within ten working days and will publish a note here when something is fixed. If you are not satisfied with our response, ask to be put in touch with the university’s [accessibility contact].

Video Communities Revisited

Video Communities Revisited is a research project on the role of physical screen media — VHS, Betamax, DVD, rental shops, collectors, swap meets and boutique labels — in forming communities in Britain, past and present.
