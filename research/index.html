Research

Research questions and work strands

4 guiding questions, organised around histories, legacies and futures.

The questions

  1. How do physical media formats shape the social life of film and television?
  2. What did video rental shops, clubs and swap meets do for their neighbourhoods, and what survives of them?
  3. How do collectors, archivists and enthusiasts keep formats usable today?
  4. What futures are being built for physical media, from boutique Blu-ray labels to community archiving?

Histories, legacies, futures

Theme 1

Histories

Where video culture came from: rental shops and their staff, tape trading, fan networks, the first wave of domestic recording.

  • How were video communities organised before the internet?
  • What do former shop owners and staff remember about their customers?

Theme 2

Legacies

What physical media left behind: collections, skills, buildings, memories and the informal archives that hold them.

  • What happens to a collection when its collector stops collecting?
  • How do libraries, museums and archives treat video today?

Theme 3

Futures

Where physical media is going: boutique labels, restoration, format obsolescence and the work of keeping tape playable.

  • Who is still pressing discs in Britain, and for whom?
  • What would a sustainable future for tape preservation look like?

Video Communities Revisited

Video Communities Revisited is a research project on the role of physical screen media — VHS, Betamax, DVD, rental shops, collectors, swap meets and boutique labels — in forming communities in Britain, past and present.
