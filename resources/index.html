Glossary of formats

A working glossary of the formats, equipment and terms that come up in interviews and in the archive. Corrections and additions are welcome — email us or open an issue.

VHS Video Home System1976 – late 2000s
JVC's consumer cassette format, and the one that won the British rental market. Standard play (SP) and long play (LP) modes trade picture quality for running time.
Betamax Betamax1975 – 2002
Sony's rival cassette format. Smaller tapes, often better picture quality, but a shorter running time and a smaller rental library in Britain.
VHS-C VHS Compact1982 – 1990s
Compact cassette for camcorders that plays back in a VHS machine through an adapter shell. Common source of home recordings.
S-VHS Super VHS1987 – 2000s
Higher-bandwidth version of VHS with a separate luminance and chrominance signal. S-VHS tapes record and play on VHS machines with reduced quality.
U-matic U-matic1971 – 1990s
3/4-inch cassette used by broadcasters and industrial users before Betacam. Frequently the only surviving format for early video art and local television.
LaserDisc LaserDisc1978 – 2000s
Analogue video on a 12-inch optical disc. Never mainstream in Britain, but a collector format valued for commentaries and letterboxed transfers.
VCD Video CD1993 – 2000s
MPEG-1 video on a CD. Popular in parts of Asia and among diaspora communities; limited running time and quality.
DVD Digital Versatile Disc1997 – present
The format that ended tape rental in Britain. Region coding and copy protection shaped what could be bought, sold and watched.
Blu-ray / 4K UHD Blu-ray Disc and Ultra HD Blu-ray2006 – present
Optical successors to DVD, now largely a collector and boutique-label market in Britain, often sold in limited numbered editions.
Sticky-shed syndrome Sticky-shed syndrome
Binder breakdown in magnetic tape that makes it squeal and shed oxide. Treated by careful cleaning and controlled baking before transfer.

Reading list

A starter reading list. Citations are given in full so they can be followed up; links go to publisher or open-access copies where they exist.

Books

  • Greenberg, Joshua M. (2008) From Betamax to Blockbuster: Video Stores and the Invention of Movies on Video. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
  • Wasser, Frederick (2001) Veni, Vidi, Video: The Hollywood Empire and the VCR. Austin: University of Texas Press.
  • Klinger, Barbara (2006) Beyond the Multiplex: Cinema, New Technologies, and the Home. Berkeley: University of California Press.
  • Herbert, Daniel (2014) Videoland: Movie Culture at the American Video Store. Berkeley: University of California Press.
  • Benson-Allott, Caetlin (2013) Killer Tapes and Shattered Screens: Video Spectatorship from VHS to File Sharing. Berkeley: University of California Press.
  • Hilderbrand, Lucas (2009) Inherent Vice: Bootleg Histories of Videotape and Copyright. Durham, NC: Duke University Press.

Teaching and workshop material

Material for seminars, workshops and community groups. All of it can be used as it is; we are happy to run a session with your group.

Video shop memory sheet

Community group or classroom, 30–45 minutes

A one-page prompt sheet for recording memories of a local shop: staff, stock, opening hours, the regulars, the moment it closed.

Reading a tape

Undergraduate seminar

Exercise on treating a videotape as an object: case, label, hand-written annotations, and what a playback check can and cannot tell you.

Mapping your local video culture

Workshop, 90 minutes

Participants plot shops, clubs and swap meets they remember, then compare notes and check against directories. Produces map entries we can add with consent.

Video Communities Revisited

Video Communities Revisited is a research project on the role of physical screen media — VHS, Betamax, DVD, rental shops, collectors, swap meets and boutique labels — in forming communities in Britain, past and present.
