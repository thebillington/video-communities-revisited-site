Where the data comes from

Sources

The map is assembled from directories that already exist. This page names them, says what we took and under what terms, and points you at the places where you can add something yourself.

Two rules govern everything below. We take facts - names, towns, addresses, dates - and not other people's prose or photographs. And we link back, so the people who did the work get the credit and the traffic.

Where a source can be collected from automatically we do that, slowly and politely, obeying its robots.txt. Where it cannot - because it is a closed social group, or behind a login, or because the site asks automated visitors not to come - a person reads it and enters the records by hand.

The Ham and Egger Files

Terms All rights reserved. Used for research with attribution and a link back.

A monthly survey of video shops still trading in Britain, with notes on former shops, ghost signs and video-shop-themed venues. The closest thing to a live national directory that exists.

open data · collected automatically

Companies House

Terms Open Government Licence v3.0 (contains public sector information)

The register of every company that has ever traded in Britain. The coverage layer of the dataset: SIC 77220 "Renting of video tapes and disks" and its predecessor 77182 "Video rental" identify video rental businesses, and each record carries the registered address and the incorporation and dissolution dates. Collected with tools/fetch_companies_house.py, which uses the official API when a production key is available.

Add a place yourself

If a shop, club, swap meet, archive or collection is missing, we want to hear about it - and we would rather hear about it from you than find it in a search engine. The contribution form asks for the name, the town, roughly when it traded, and whatever you remember. The map is the quickest way to see what is already there.

If you maintain one of the directories above and would rather we did not scrape it, say so and we will stop and ask the people who run it instead.

Video Communities Revisited

Video Communities Revisited is a research project on the role of physical screen media — VHS, Betamax, DVD, rental shops, collectors, swap meets and boutique labels — in forming communities in Britain, past and present.
